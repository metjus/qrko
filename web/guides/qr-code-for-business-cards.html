QR code for business cards

One scan and your contact details are saved on the phone. Here is what belongs in the code, what size still scans and where to place it.

A paper business card has one problem: it ends up in a drawer and the contact never reaches anyone's phone. A contact QR code solves that — the person scans it, confirms, and you are in their address book with name, number and email already filled in.

What the code contains

It uses the vCard format — an electronic business card understood by Android phones, iPhones and email programs alike. After scanning, the phone offers to save a new contact with the fields already populated.

As with a Wi-Fi code, the details live inside the pattern itself. The code therefore works permanently and does not depend on any service that might one day disappear.

Which details to include

The rule is simple: the fewer details, the simpler the pattern and the more reliably it scans. Every extra line increases the density of the code.

  • Full name — the only required field.
  • Phone — ideally in international format with the country code, e.g. +44 7700 900000. The contact then works for people abroad too.
  • Email — the most used detail after the phone number.
  • Company and job title — useful if you want the person to place your name in context months later.
  • Website — worth adding if your site explains what you do.

What you can safely leave out: a full postal address, several phone numbers and social media handles. They make the code denser, harder to scan, and most people never use them.

What size still scans

This is the most common question, and the answer depends on two things — how much data the code holds and from what distance it is scanned.

  • On a business card, aim for at least 15 × 15 mm, ideally 18 × 18 mm. Anything smaller with a lot of data is a gamble.
  • More data means a denser pattern and a bigger code. A code with only a name and phone number is far sparser than one with six fields.
  • A common rule of thumb: the code should be roughly one tenth of the distance it is scanned from. You hold a card about 20 cm from the phone, so a 2 cm code is a safe bet.

Where to place it on the card

The back of the card is usually best — on its own, with clear space around it. The front belongs to your name and logo. If the code has to go on the front, put it in a corner and let white space breathe around it.

Critically, keep the code away from the edge. Trimming during production has a tolerance of a few millimetres, and a code touching the edge can end up damaged. Leave at least 5 mm of margin.

Contrast and colour

Scanners recognise codes by the contrast between dark and light modules. So:

  • Classic black on white is the most reliable.
  • If you want colour, the dark modules must be markedly darker than the background. Navy on white works; light grey on white does not.
  • Never invert the colours — a light code on a dark background. Many scanners will not recognise it at all.
  • Do not place the code over a photograph or a patterned background.

Test before you order the print run

Business cards are printed in the hundreds, so a mistake is expensive. Before you order:

  • Print one test card on an ordinary printer at the final size.
  • Scan the code from paper, not from a screen — paper and screens behave differently.
  • Check that the contact saves with the correct accented characters in the name.
  • Try it on at least two different phones.
