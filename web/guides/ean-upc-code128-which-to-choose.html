EAN-13, UPC-A or CODE128 — which to choose

The three most common formats solve three different situations. Here is how to decide based on where the code is actually going.

Before you generate a barcode, it helps to know which type your situation actually calls for. Picking the wrong format isn't just cosmetic — it simply won't scan at a till or won't be accepted by a partner's system.

EAN-13 — the retail standard for Europe

This is the standard you see on every product in a Slovak or other European shop. It has exactly 13 digits, the last one being a check digit calculated from the others.

Choose EAN-13 if:

  • you sell a physical product in a shop or an online store that connects to standard retail systems,
  • your buyer (a chain, a wholesaler) explicitly requires a barcode,
  • you want to list the product on platforms like Google Shopping or price comparison sites, which often require EAN codes.

Important: for actual retail sale you need an officially assigned EAN-13, not just any 13-digit number that happens to pass the checksum. More in How to get an official barcode for your product.

UPC-A — the US counterpart to EAN-13

UPC-A is essentially the same thing as EAN-13, just with 12 digits instead of 13 (technically it's EAN-13 with a leading zero that UPC-A doesn't display). It's used mainly in the US and Canada.

Choose UPC-A if:

  • you sell into the US market, for example via Amazon.com or physically into a US chain,
  • your US buyer specifically asks for this format instead of EAN-13.

If you only sell in Europe, EAN-13 is the more appropriate choice — it's more common and more widely accepted here.

CODE128 — the general-purpose logistics code

CODE128 isn't tied to retail products at all. It handles letters, digits and punctuation (though not accented characters), which is exactly why it's used wherever you need to encode more than just a number.

Choose CODE128 if:

  • you're labelling internal tracking — a warehouse shelf, a shipment, an inventory number,
  • you need to combine letters and digits, for example a product serial number,
  • the code never passes through a regular shop till — it's for your own internal system or a partner's system that supports CODE128.

Unlike EAN-13/UPC-A, CODE128 needs no registration anywhere — you can put whatever you need into it, since it doesn't serve as a global product identifier.

Quick reference

SituationRecommended type
Product for a Slovak/European shopEAN-13
Product for the US marketUPC-A
Warehouse or internal trackingCODE128
Serial number with letters and digitsCODE128
A code that goes through a shop tillEAN-13 or UPC-A
