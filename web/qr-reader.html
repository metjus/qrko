How to scan a QR code from an image or screenshot

Use this online QR code scanner with a saved picture. It reads images rather than a live camera feed; on a phone, you can choose a camera photo as the input.

  1. Add the picture. Drag it onto the box above, click the box to choose a file, or paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac). On a phone, pick the camera option to photograph a code on paper.
  2. Wait a moment. The reader looks for QR codes in your picture, trying several sizes and sections of it, and finds up to six codes.
  3. Check the result. You see what the code contains. Links are never opened automatically, and we point out anything suspicious about them, such as shortened or unencrypted addresses.
  4. Copy or open it. Copy the text, save a contact card, or open the link if you trust it.

What can a QR code contain?

The reader recognises the most common kinds and shows each one in a readable way.

  • Web links, the most common kind, shown with the website name and safety hints.
  • Wi-Fi networks: network name, security type and password.
  • Contact cards (vCard) with name, phone, email and more, which you can save as a file.
  • Email addresses, phone numbers and SMS messages.
  • Map locations given as coordinates.
  • Plain text and data meant for other apps, such as payments.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to the questions people ask most often.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. The picture is read by JavaScript running in your own browser. It is not sent to our server or to anyone else, and nothing is stored.

Which image formats work?

PNG, JPG, WebP and GIF pictures work in modern browsers, as do screenshots and phone photos. Some formats, such as HEIC photos from an iPhone, are not supported by every browser. If your browser cannot open a picture, take a screenshot of it or save it as JPG or PNG.

Why can't the reader find my QR code?

Crop the picture so the code fills most of it, and make sure the whole code is visible, including the white border around it. Blur, glare and shadows make codes hard to read, so a sharper and larger picture helps. For a code on a screen, a screenshot works better than a photo of the screen.

Is it safe to open a link from a QR code?

Not always. A QR code hides the address, so criminals sometimes put harmful codes on stickers, posters or emails. That is why this reader shows you the address first and never opens it for you. Check the domain name carefully, be wary of shortened links and of links that ask for passwords or payments, and when in doubt type the website's address yourself.

Can it read more than one QR code in a picture?

Yes. If a picture contains several QR codes, for example on a flyer or a sheet of stickers, the reader shows each one separately, up to six per picture.

Can it read barcodes such as EAN or UPC?

No, this reader handles QR codes only. If you need to create EAN-13, UPC-A or CODE128 barcodes, use the barcode generator.

Can I scan with my camera live?

Not live. This page reads pictures. On a phone, choose a file and pick the camera option to photograph a code, or simply point your phone's own camera app at it, which reads QR codes on most modern phones.
